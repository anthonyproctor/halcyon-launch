# Danny's Logbook: Private Pilot course bible

Read this whole file before writing anything. Also read `../../full/BIBLE.md` (the PMP course bible) for the shared format, voice rules, answer-length rules, flags, props, and `data-who` speaker tags, and `../../full/ch01.js` for the exact object shape and voice. This course reuses that engine and those rules unless this file says otherwise.

## Goal
A 12 to 15 hour story-driven companion course for the FAA **Private Pilot Airplane (PAR) knowledge test**. It is a companion to Anthony's ground school (Sporty's), not a replacement. Each chapter takes about 60 to 75 minutes: a story about a man learning to fly his late brother's airplane that you can't put down, a lesson that actually teaches the ACS knowledge elements, decisions in the cockpit and on the ground, E6B style math drills, a chart or weather exercise, and a 15-question test-style quiz. A reader who finishes should pass the PAR knowledge test and walk into the checkride oral understanding the why.

## The test (verified 2026-10-09)
- **Private Pilot Airplane (PAR) knowledge test:** 60 scored questions, plus up to 5 unscored validation questions (about 65 on screen). **2.0 hours** (reduced from 150 minutes effective April 24, 2023). **Passing score 70 percent** (42 of 60). Multiple choice, three options (A, B, C) on the real test.
- **Standard:** Private Pilot Airplane Airman Certification Standards, **FAA-S-ACS-6C** (effective May 31, 2024). Some older material still lists 2.5 hours; it's stale.
- **Supplement:** questions reference the Airman Knowledge Testing Supplement (FAA-CT-8080-2, figures: sectional excerpts, METAR/TAF, performance charts, weight and balance, airport diagrams). Our props and drills mimic those figures.
- **Primary references:** Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (PHAK, FAA-H-8083-25), Airplane Flying Handbook (AFH, FAA-H-8083-3), Aviation Weather Handbook (FAA-H-8083-28), Aeronautical Information Manual (AIM), 14 CFR Parts 61 and 91, NTSB 830, Risk Management Handbook (FAA-H-8083-2).
- Sources:
  - FAA community advisory on reduced test times: https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/community_advisory_april_2023
  - FAA ACS knowledge test table: https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/acs/acs_knowledge_test_table.pdf
  - Private Pilot Airplane ACS: https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/acs/private_airplane_acs_6.pdf
  - Exam facts summary (cross check): https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/faa-private-pilot/introduction/exam-facts
  - PHAK: https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak

### Important format difference from PMP
The real PAR test uses **three** options. Our decisions keep four options (story choices), but **quiz questions and the mock exam use three options (A, B, C)**. The validator for this course checks 3-option quiz items; answer-length and position rules apply the same way (right answer not reliably longest, positions balanced).

## ACS areas of operation and knowledge codes (FAA-S-ACS-6C)
Every quiz question and mock question is tagged with one of these task codes. Use the ACS element style in `why` text where useful (for example PA.I.C.K3).
- **PA.I Preflight Preparation:** I.A Pilot Qualifications · I.B Airworthiness Requirements · I.C Weather Information · I.D Cross-Country Flight Planning · I.E National Airspace System · I.F Performance and Limitations · I.G Operation of Systems · I.H Human Factors
- **PA.II Preflight Procedures:** II.A Preflight Assessment · II.B Flight Deck Management · II.C Engine Starting · II.D Taxiing · II.F Before Takeoff Check
- **PA.III Airport Operations:** III.A Communications, Light Signals, and Runway Lighting Systems · III.B Traffic Patterns
- **PA.IV Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds:** normal, crosswind, soft-field, short-field, forward slip, go-around
- **PA.V Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers:** steep turns, ground reference maneuvers
- **PA.VI Navigation:** VI.A Pilotage and Dead Reckoning · VI.B Navigation Systems and Radar Services · VI.C Diversion · VI.D Lost Procedures
- **PA.VII Slow Flight and Stalls:** slow flight, power-off and power-on stalls, spin awareness
- **PA.VIII Basic Instrument Maneuvers**
- **PA.IX Emergency Operations:** emergency descent, emergency approach and landing, systems and equipment malfunctions, emergency equipment and survival gear
- **PA.XI Night Operations**
- **PA.XII Postflight Procedures**
Weighting for the written: Preflight Preparation (I.A to I.H) dominates real tests, especially regulations, airspace, weather, performance, and systems. Mirror that: about 60 percent of quiz and mock items from Area I, the rest spread across III, IV, VI, VII, IX, XI, and aeromedical/ADM.

Task codes for tagging (use exactly these strings): `I.A` `I.B` `I.C` `I.D` `I.E` `I.F` `I.G` `I.H` `II` `III.A` `III.B` `IV` `V` `VI` `VII` `VIII` `IX` `XI` `XII`. Domain field for each item: `Preflight` (I.x, II), `Airport and flight` (III, IV, V, VII, VIII, XII), `Navigation and emergencies` (VI, IX, XI).

## Answer quality rules
Identical to the PMP bible, plus:
- **Safety first, always.** The best answer is the conservative, regulation-compliant, risk-managed choice. Never teach or reward anything unsafe or illegal. When a distractor is unsafe, the `why` says plainly why it could hurt someone.
- **Exact regulations.** Cite the specific 14 CFR section or AIM paragraph in lesson text or `why` when it settles the answer (for example 91.155 basic VFR weather minimums, 61.57 recency, 91.103 preflight action, 91.205 required equipment, 91.211 oxygen, 91.17 alcohol). Double check every number against the source.
- **Exact math.** Show formula and steps. Use standard conventions: density altitude rule of thumb DA ≈ PA + 120 × (OAT minus ISA temp), ISA = 15°C minus 2°C per 1,000 ft; pressure altitude = field elevation + (29.92 minus altimeter) × 1,000; crosswind = wind speed × sin(angle) using the clock rule or exact sine; weight and balance moment = weight × arm, CG = total moment ÷ total weight; time = distance ÷ groundspeed; fuel = time × burn rate plus required reserve (91.151: 30 minutes day, 45 minutes night). Use realistic 1978 Cessna 172N numbers (Lycoming O-320 160 hp, carbureted; basic empty weight about 1,450 lb; max gross 2,300 lb normal category; 40 gal usable standard tanks; about 8 gal/hr cruise burn). Pull every chart value from a stated table in the chapter, never from memory.
- No em or en dashes. Contractions. Plain words. Dialogue in `<span class="said" data-who="Name">"..."</span>`.

## Cast and arcs
Voice and tone: plain, specific, dry humor, the way The Phoenix Project handles people. Grief carries real weight in specific scenes and objects (a headset, a coffee can of spare fuel caps, a voicemail he won't delete), never speeches or melodrama. Every main character has an arc that pays off by chapter 12. Nobody is old or slow. Jordan learns fast; his problem is never ability, it's what he's carrying.

- **Jordan Reyes** (you). 35, a man. Commercial HVAC project estimator in Aurora: reads mechanical drawings for a living, quick with numbers, good at spotting what a plan forgot. Learns fast and knows it, which is part of the problem. Has never flown. His older brother Danny was the one who did the exciting things; Jordan was the one who did the sensible things. Flaw: he either avoids anything that reminds him of Danny or tries to do it exactly the way Danny would have, including Danny's habit of making the trip work. Arc: from flying Danny's airplane to flying his own decisions in it. Learns that respecting his brother doesn't mean copying him, and that the best pilots are the ones who decide not to go.
- **Danny Reyes** (present only through the logbook, voicemails, and other people's stories). Died at 41 of a sudden cardiac arrest at his daughter's Saturday soccer game. Nothing to do with flying. Private pilot for eleven years. Funny, generous, a little too confident. Left Jordan his quarter share in the airplane with a note in the will: "Learn to fly it or sell it to Walt. Don't let it sit." His handwritten logbook comes with the share.
- **The logbook (the second teacher).** One Danny entry per chapter, tied to that chapter's topic, shown as a prop (type "doc", handwriting style). The remarks column is where Danny wrote what he learned: a carb ice scare (ch2), a TAF he misread (ch3), a Class B clearance he forgot to get (ch4), a hot day takeoff that ate most of the runway (ch5), and so on. Most entries are funny and a little self-mocking. The ch10 entry is not: Danny pressed into lowering weather on a night flight home, got lucky, and wrote "Got lucky. Never again. Don't tell Mom." The last entry in the logbook is a planned trip he never flew (see ch12).
- **Abby Strand.** 28, CFI at the club. Building hours for an airline job and counting them out loud. Blunt and funny ("I don't do pep talks. I do checklists."). Knew Danny a little: he was the club member who brought her breakfast burritos the week her first student failed a checkride and told her it wasn't her fault. Arc: she treats Jordan as hours to log until he becomes the student who reminds her why she likes teaching. In ch12 she gets her regional airline offer, and she pushes her start date back a week so she can watch his checkride takeoff from the ramp.
- **Walt Herrera.** 64, one of the four partners, retired airline dispatcher, club safety officer. Wants Jordan's share sold back because he doesn't think a grieving man who's never flown should own a quarter of an airplane, and he says so. He isn't wrong, and he isn't cruel. Years ago Walt dispatched a flight that didn't come home; he never talks about it until ch10. Arc: the doubter becomes the man who signs Jordan up for the checkride slot and stands at the fence. His safety talks are the course's ADM spine.
- **Marisol Tran.** 44, partner and A&P mechanic, Danny's closest friend at the airport. Teaches systems from the hangar floor with the cowling off. Grieving quietly and badly; she keeps fixing things on the airplane that don't need fixing. Arc: she lets Jordan in, tells him the stories about Danny that his family never heard, and in ch7 gives him Danny's headset, which she's been keeping in her toolbox.
- **Lourdes Reyes.** 62, Jordan and Danny's mother. Hates the airplane; to her it's the thing that almost took Danny for years and then didn't. Asks Jordan not to do this. Arc: from "please don't" to standing on the ramp in ch12. Her phone calls are short and loaded.
- **Claire Reyes.** 40, Danny's widow, a nurse at a Denver ER. Practical, tired, honest. The airplane share cost money every month and Danny's time on weekends, and she has feelings about that. She's the one who tells Jordan, in ch8, what Danny was like when he got "go fever." Arc: from resenting the airplane to deciding Ellie can fly with Jordan.
- **Ellie Reyes.** 9, Danny's daughter. Asks the best questions in the course ("If the air is thinner, does the airplane get tired?"), which double as teaching beats for density altitude and lift. Wants to sit in Daddy's seat.
- **The fourth partner, Russ Kim.** 50, travels for work, mostly appears in the club group chat with scheduling fights and bad puns. Comic relief and a realistic source of airplane scheduling and maintenance squawk props.
- **Kendra Lowe.** The designated pilot examiner (DPE) at the checkride (ch12). Calm, exacting, kind at the end.

## Fixed facts
- **The airplane: N4791E, a 1978 Cessna 172N** owned by the four-partner Hangar Six Flying Club (fictional) at **Centennial Airport (KAPA)**. Carbureted Lycoming O-320, six-pack analog gauges, an older nav/com with a VOR head, a club-installed panel GPS and ADS-B Out (required here under the Denver Class B and its Mode C veil). The course teaches the analog instruments the test still covers. Its quirks are characters: a sticky fuel selector, a door that pops open on rotation if you don't latch the top, a tachometer that reads 50 RPM high. Danny named it "Biscuit."
- **The deal:** the other three partners give Jordan one year to earn his private pilot certificate and fly it, or sell the share back at book value. The clock is real and comes up in ch1, ch6, ch9 and ch12.
- Home airport **KAPA Centennial** (field elevation about 5,885 ft MSL, towered Class D, underlies the Denver Class B). Practice area east of the airport over the plains. Alternate fields: **KBJC Rocky Mountain Metropolitan**, **KFTG Front Range**, **KCOS** (Class C), **KLMO Vance Brand**, **KGXY Greeley**, **KAKO Colorado Plains Regional (Akron)**.
- Density altitude is a constant character: summer afternoons at KAPA push density altitude above 8,000 ft. The mountains to the west mean turbulence, mountain wave, and fast-building afternoon thunderstorms.
- Jordan's first solo is ch7, first solo cross-country ch9, knowledge test ch11, checkride ch12.
- **Danny's last logbook entry** is a planned, never-flown trip written in pencil: "KAPA to KAKO. Pie at the diner. Take Ellie when she's tall enough to see over the panel. Bring Jordan." That route is the final flight.
- Never cite real accidents by name or real people. Fictional NTSB-style anecdotes only, clearly fictional.
- No Ruth Calder, no Sam Okafor, no Halcyon characters in this course.

## Chapter plan (num, title, content, ACS codes, drills and exercises, logbook entry, ending)
1. **Discovery Flight.** The will, the note, the share, the one-year clock. Walt says plainly he wants it sold. Jordan books a discovery flight to prove to himself he can say no. Meets Abby and Biscuit. Pilot certification path (student pilot certificate, medical classes, 61.103 eligibility, 61.109 hours), the four forces, airplane parts and controls. Decisions: which medical, choosing an instructor, how to answer Walt. Exercise: match controls to axes. Codes I.A, I.G. Logbook: Danny's first lesson, eleven years ago ("Instructor says I'm a natural. Instructor says that to everyone."). Ends: after landing, Jordan sits in the airplane alone for ten minutes and doesn't tell anyone why.
2. **The Machine.** Systems with Marisol, cowling off: four-stroke engine, magnetos, carburetor and carb ice, mixture, fuel system (and that sticky selector), electrical, pitot-static instruments and gyros, failure indications. Airworthiness: 91.205 required equipment (TOMATO FLAMES), 91.213 inoperative equipment, AROW documents, required inspections (annual, 100 hour, ELT, transponder, pitot-static). Jordan's estimator brain finds a missed item in the maintenance records; Marisol is furious, at herself. Drill: hours to next 100 hour inspection; can it fly with this item inop. Codes I.B, I.G. Logbook: a carb ice scare. Ends: Marisol tells Jordan the first story about Danny he's never heard.
3. **Weather School.** METAR, TAF, winds aloft, prog charts, AIRMETs and SIGMETs, PIREPs, fronts, stability, thunderstorms, fog, icing, mountain wave. Jordan cancels a lesson for wind and Abby, deadpan, says it's the first smart thing he's done. Ellie asks why clouds are flat on the bottom. Props: real-format KAPA METARs and TAFs. Exercise: decode a METAR and TAF line by line. Drill: cloud base from temperature and dewpoint spread. Codes I.C. Logbook: a TAF Danny misread. Ends: an afternoon thunderstorm over the Palmer Divide, watched from Claire's kitchen window.
4. **Airspace.** Classes A through G, Denver Class B shelves, the Mode C veil and ADS-B Out, KAPA Class D, VFR weather minimums (91.155), special use airspace, TFRs, NOTAMs. Radio work with Centennial Tower and Denver Approach. Props: a sectional excerpt description, a TFR NOTAM, a club chat fight about the schedule. Exercise: sectional chart reading. Codes I.E, III.A. Logbook: Danny forgot a Class B clearance and got a phone number to call. Ends: Jordan nearly climbs into the Class B shelf; Abby catches it, and he realizes he did it the same way Danny did.
5. **Performance.** Density altitude, takeoff and landing distance charts, climb performance, weight and balance, CG effects. A hot August afternoon. Ellie: "Does the airplane get tired?" Drills: density altitude; weight and balance with Jordan, Abby, and fuel; takeoff distance from a POH table with a headwind correction. Codes I.F. Logbook: a hot day takeoff that used most of the runway. Ends: Jordan does the math, tells Ellie she can't come on this flight, and she cries, and he's right.
6. **Pattern Work.** Traffic patterns, right of way (91.113), wake turbulence, runway markings and lighting, light gun signals, crosswind takeoffs and landings, go-arounds, forward slips. Six months gone on the clock. Walt watches from the fence. Drill: crosswind and headwind components for KAPA runways. Exercise: runway markings and light gun signals. Codes III.B, III.A, IV. Logbook: Danny's ugliest landing, drawn as a cartoon. Ends: Abby climbs out, signs his logbook, and says "Three. Full stop. Don't make it weird."
7. **First Solo.** Pre-solo knowledge test, 61.87 solo requirements and endorsements, slow flight, stalls, spin awareness, steep turns, ground reference maneuvers. The solo, told minute by minute. Marisol gives him Danny's headset. Props: the solo endorsement. Codes VII, V, I.A. Logbook: Danny's solo ("Talked to myself the whole time. Tower heard some of it."). Ends: the shirt tail, and Jordan calls his mother, who doesn't pick up, and then calls back.
8. **Human Factors.** Aeromedical (hypoxia, hyperventilation, spatial disorientation, carbon monoxide, alcohol and drugs, 91.17, fatigue, middle ear, motion sickness), IMSAFE, PAVE, 3P, hazardous attitudes and antidotes, ADM, SRM. Grief itself as an IMSAFE item: Jordan flies a lesson the day before Danny's birthday and it goes badly. Claire tells him about Danny's "go fever." Exercise: match hazardous attitudes to antidotes. Codes I.H. Logbook: a fatigue entry. Ends: Jordan writes his personal minimums on an index card and tapes it to the panel where Danny kept a photo of Ellie.
9. **Cross-Country.** Pilotage and dead reckoning, VFR nav log, true course to compass heading (variation, deviation, wind correction), VOR, GPS, flight following, diversion and lost procedures, fuel planning (91.151). Solo cross-country KAPA to KGXY to KFTG. Drills: a nav log leg; time-speed-distance diversion. Props: the nav log and the flight plan. Codes I.D, VI. Logbook: Danny's first cross-country. Ends: a diversion around building buildups, handled well, and Jordan realizes he didn't think about Danny once, and feels guilty, and then doesn't.
10. **The No-Go.** Night operations (61.57 night currency, lighting, illusions, night vision) and emergencies (engine failure, electrical failure, fire, emergency landings, ELT, 121.5). The emotional center: Lourdes's birthday dinner, a beautiful evening, the family waiting at the other end, marginal weather moving in. Jordan finds Danny's "Got lucky. Never again. Don't tell Mom." entry the same night. Walt finally tells the dispatch story. Codes XI, IX, I.H. Ends: Jordan stays on the ground and drives. His mother, at dinner, says it's the first time a Reyes boy ever turned around.
11. **The Written.** Regulations review (61 and 91 essentials, NTSB 830, logbook requirements, 61.113 privileges and limitations, 61.56 flight review, 61.57 recency), test strategy, test day at a testing center. Exercise: which regulation answers this question. Codes I.A, I.B, XII. Logbook: Danny's knowledge test score, which Jordan beats, and it feels strange. Ends: the score and the areas he missed (these feed the study plan).
12. **Checkride.** The oral and the flight with Kendra Lowe, postflight procedures. Abby's airline offer arrives the same week. Walt books the slot and stands at the fence. Lourdes on the ramp. Then the last logbook entry: KAPA to KAKO with Ellie in the right seat on a booster cushion so she can see over the panel. Codes II, XII, I.A, IV. Ends: pie at the diner in Akron, and Jordan writes the first entry in his own logbook's remarks column. Mirror chapter 1's ten quiet minutes in the airplane, but this time he tells Ellie why.

## Props (same format as the PMP bible)
Use 1 to 3 per chapter: METAR and TAF lines (type "doc" with monospace content), NOTAM and TFR text, the logbook endorsement (type "doc"), nav log (type "doc"), POH performance table (type "doc" or chart kind "bars"), weight and balance worksheet (type "doc"), texts from Lourdes, Claire and Ellie (type "text"), Danny's logbook entries (type "doc"), club chat messages (type "chat").

## Quiz and mock exam
- **Chapter quiz:** 15 questions, **three options** (A, B, C), tagged with the task codes above and a domain. Test style: concise stem, often referencing a figure-like prop or a number to compute.
- **Mock exam:** 60 questions, three options, 2.0 hours, weighted like the real test (about 60 percent Area I, with heavy regulations, airspace, weather, performance, systems, and ADM), including at least 15 calculation questions (density altitude, pressure altitude, weight and balance and CG shifts, crosswind, time-speed-distance, fuel, cloud base, true course to compass heading). Neutral fictional settings, not the Jordan story. Passing 70 percent.
